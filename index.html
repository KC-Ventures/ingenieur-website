Ingenieur Labs

Some ideas arrive with one dimension too many.

They're too big to hold, too vague to start, or a little ahead of their time. We take them apart, find the piece that works today, and build it until it's something people can actually use.

Every project here starts as a hunch and ends as working software.

From a hunch to a company.

  1. Notice

    It starts with a problem one of us keeps running into. If we wouldn't use the fix ourselves, we don't build it.

  2. Prototype

    A working version in weeks, not a pitch deck. Rough is fine. Real is required.

  3. Ship

    We put it in front of people who have the same problem and watch what they actually do with it.

  4. Spin out

    When it has users and momentum, it becomes a company of its own.

Most ideas stop after the first or second step. We keep what we learned and start the next one sooner.

Small on purpose.

Ingenieur Labs is two friends who would rather build than talk about building. We choose the problems, write the code, ship it, and decide together which projects deserve to become companies.

Ingenieur is German for engineer. The word traces back to the Latin ingenium, which also gave us ingenious. That's the bar.
